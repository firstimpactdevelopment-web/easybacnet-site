Short answer

The AppStat is a wall-mounted BACnet MS/TP room controller (thermostat style) with fixed applications for fan coils, rooftop units and heat pumps. Its objects cover inputs (room, remote, discharge and outdoor or water temperature, humidity, motion), the outputs for the chosen application, setpoints, operating modes, a schedule and PID loops.

BMS integration notes: KMC Controls BAC-4000 AppStat, heat pump application

Integration in brief

Set the device instance, MAC address and baud on the AppStat, then connect it to BACnet MS/TP. Occupancy comes from schedule object SCHED_1 writing BV5 OCC_SCHEDULE, so either use that schedule or write BV5 from the BMS; read space temperature, the active setpoints and fan status.

Enable & remote start

Occupancy state is held in BV5 OCC_SCHEDULE, the reference of the controller's own schedule SCHED_1 (holiday schedules are its exception schedule).

Sensors & values from the BMS

A remote room sensor can be wired to AI1 REMOTE_SENSOR; the guide does not describe writing room temperature over BACnet.

Network setup

  • Set device instance, MAC address and baud on the AppStat (Set up communications section) before connecting.
  • Max 128 addressable devices per MS/TP network; KMC suggests 60 controllers or fewer for traffic.
  • KMD-5575 repeater every 32 devices or 4000 ft.

Watch out for

  • Changing object configuration can cause unpredictable operation; KMC warns against it.
  • Not every object exists on every AppStat model.

Integration notes

  • Outputs are from Table 7-5 of the guide; inputs, values, schedule and loops are shared by all AppStat applications. Not every object is present in every model.
  • The guide has no read/write column; access is inferred (setpoints and modes writable, measured and calculated values read-only, outputs commandable).
  • AI2 is WATER_TEMP on fan coil models and OAT on heat pump and rooftop models.
  • No units or multi-state texts are printed in the object tables.
  • AV13-AV18 (discharge air reset, economizer and compressor/aux heat lockouts) are the economizer and heat pump setpoints.

Questions about the KMC Controls BAC-4000 AppStat, heat pump application

How many BACnet points does the KMC Controls BAC-4000 AppStat, heat pump application have?

This list has 48 BACnet objects: 5 analog inputs, 1 analog output, 23 analog values, 1 binary input, 5 binary outputs, 5 binary values, 4 loops, 1 schedule, 3 multi-state values. The exact set on a given unit depends on its firmware, installed options and configuration.

Which KMC Controls BAC-4000 AppStat, heat pump application BACnet points are writable?

29 of the 48 objects accept a write. Points marked Command have a priority array: a write holds until it is released, so release it back to automatic when you are done. The source has no read/write column, so this is inferred from object type.

Where is the official KMC Controls BAC-4000 AppStat, heat pump application BACnet documentation?

The source is Installation, Operation, and Application Guide for AppStat (Revision I), revision 2025, published free by KMC Controls at https://www.kmccontrols.com/wp-content/uploads/kmc_documents/AG_BAC-4000_AppStatInstallationApplicationGuideRevI.pdf.

What are the minimum BACnet points to integrate the KMC Controls BAC-4000 AppStat, heat pump application?

Start with BV 5 OCC_SCHEDULE (Occupancy state, written by schedule SCHED_1); AV 5 OCC_CL_STPT (Occupied cooling setpoint); AV 6 OCC_HT_SPT (Occupied heating setpoint); MV 2 SYSTEM_MODE (Control (system) mode); AV 1 SPACE_TEMP (Space temperature); MV 1 OCCUPIED_MODE (Occupied mode); AV 20 DISCHARGE_TEMP (Discharge air temperature); BV 14 FAN_STATUS (Fan proof status).

How do I enable BMS control of the KMC Controls BAC-4000 AppStat, heat pump application?
