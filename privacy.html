Privacy Policy for Easy BACnet

Effective date: 2 October 2026

The short version:
  • The Easy BACnet Android app has no account, no sign-up and no server. What it reads from your building network stays on your phone and is never sent to us.
  • The app contains no analytics or crash-reporting SDK of its own.
  • The free version shows Google AdMob rewarded video ads, only when you choose to watch one (to export results or open a saved remote). Google collects your device's advertising ID and device/usage data to serve those ads.
  • The optional Easy BACnet Pro subscription is paid through Google Play. We never see your payment details; the app only stores an “active” flag on your phone.
  • This website uses Google Analytics only if you accept the cookie banner.

This policy covers the Easy BACnet app for Android (“the app”) and the easybacnet.com website. “We” and “us” means the developer of Easy BACnet. Questions go to Firstimpactdevelopment@gmail.com.

What the app does

Easy BACnet is a field tool for building-automation work. It broadcasts a standard BACnet Who-Is on the local network (and, if you set one up, through a BBMD or directly to an IP address you type in), lists the BACnet IP devices that respond, reads their points, can write to points when you turn on write mode, lets you build custom remotes and control panels, and can export the results as an Excel spreadsheet.

Information we do not collect

  • No name, email address, phone number, or account of any kind.
  • No location permission. The app never asks for it and does not read GPS.
  • No contacts, photos, or other files from your phone. The only files the app opens are ones you pick yourself when importing a remote or control panel.
  • No analytics, crash reporting, or usage statistics sent to us. We have no server, so nothing from the app reaches us at all.

Building-network data the app reads

While you use it, the app reads technical information from BACnet devices on the network you are connected to: device names, Device IDs (instance numbers), IP addresses and ports, vendor and model names, routing details for devices behind a BACnet router, point names, present values, engineering units, status flags, descriptions, and priority arrays. It also shows your phone's own IP address and subnet mask on screen when a scan finds nothing, to help you troubleshoot.

Scan results are kept in the phone's memory only while the app is running and are not saved between sessions. The session write log (the list of writes and releases you made) is also memory-only. None of this is ever sent to us or to any third party by the app; it only leaves your phone if you export or share it yourself.

Information stored on your phone

The app keeps a few things in its private storage on your phone, which other apps cannot read:

  • Saved custom remotes and control panels — the layout you built, the point each control is bound to (object type and number), labels, colours, and for each device its Device ID, name, last known IP address and port (and router network/MAC for routed devices), so the remote can reconnect without a new scan.
  • Settings — the BBMD address and port if you entered one, your chosen language, your appearance choice (dark, light or follow system), and that you accepted the terms of use.
  • Subscription and ad state — a yes/no flag recording whether Easy BACnet Pro is active, and a temporary flag that remembers a reward you earned if the screen was closed while the ad played.
  • Export files — see below.

Backup is switched off (android:allowBackup="false", with device-transfer and cloud-backup rules that exclude everything), so none of this is copied to Google Drive backup or moved to a new phone. An Easy BACnet Pro subscription is still restored on a new phone or reinstall signed in with the same Google account, because Google Play keeps track of it.

Exports and shared files

When you tap Export Results, the app writes an Excel spreadsheet (.xlsx, named like EasyBACnet_20260930_1415.xlsx) into its private cache folder. It contains, for each device and point found: Device ID, device name, device IP, object type, object number, point name, present value, units and status. The app then opens Android's share sheet with that file attached. You choose the app (email, Drive, a messenger) and the recipient; the app never sends anything by itself and has no email account of its own. Once you send it, the file is handled by the app and service you chose and by whoever receives it.

Long-pressing a saved remote or control panel exports it as an .ebremote or .ebpanel file through Android's save dialog, to a location you choose. These files contain the layout plus the Device IDs, names and IP addresses described above. Importing reads only the file you select.

Export files in the app's cache stay there until you clear the app's cache or storage, or uninstall the app. A points list describes a building's controls, so send it only to people who should have it.

The Easy BACnet Pro subscription

On the free version you can keep one custom remote and one control panel (for one device), and exporting or opening a saved remote offers a rewarded ad. Easy BACnet Pro is an optional auto-renewing subscription, billed yearly, that removes the ads and lifts those limits. It is sold through Google Play Billing: Google processes the payment and handles your payment details under the Google Privacy Policy and Google Play's terms. The app never sees or stores a card number, billing address or name. It receives only Google Play's confirmation of whether the subscription is active, stores an “active” flag on your phone, and re-checks with Google Play when it can (for example to restore Pro after a reinstall or on a new phone signed in with the same Google account, or to switch it off when the subscription ends).

You can cancel at any time in Google Play → Subscriptions; Pro stays active until the end of the year you have paid for. If the subscription ends, your saved remotes and panels stay on your phone, and the free limits and ads apply again.

Permissions and why they are needed

The app shows no permission prompts. Android grants these automatically at install:

  • INTERNET — opens the UDP socket used to speak BACnet IP on your local network, and lets Google Play and AdMob reach Google. Android requires it for any network socket, even a local-only one.
  • ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE — finds the Wi-Fi or Ethernet network to scan on (and avoids sending BACnet traffic over mobile data or a VPN), and shows your phone's IP and mask when a scan finds nothing.
  • ACCESS_WIFI_STATE — reads the Wi-Fi connection details so the scan reaches the right subnet.
  • CHANGE_WIFI_MULTICAST_STATE — lets the phone receive the broadcast replies BACnet devices send back; Android filters these out by default.
  • com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID and ACCESS_ADSERVICES_AD_ID, ACCESS_ADSERVICES_ATTRIBUTION, ACCESS_ADSERVICES_TOPICS — added by the Google Mobile Ads SDK so it can read the advertising ID and use Android's Privacy Sandbox ad APIs for the rewarded ads described above.
  • com.android.vending.BILLING — added by Google Play Billing for the Easy BACnet Pro subscription.
  • WAKE_LOCK and FOREGROUND_SERVICE — added by Google's libraries bundled with the ads SDK, which use them for their own short background tasks. The app itself runs no background service and does nothing when it is closed.

The app does not request location, camera, microphone, contacts, storage, phone or notification permissions.

Keeping, deleting and controlling your data

  • Delete one remote or panel: open it, tap Edit, then Delete remote / Delete panel in the menu.
  • Delete everything the app stored: Settings → Apps → Easy BACnet → Storage → Clear storage, or uninstall the app. Either removes saved remotes and panels, settings, the BBMD address, cached exports and the local subscription flag (the subscription itself stays on your Google account and is restored on reinstall).
  • Ad data held by Google: use Ad privacy choices in the app (where offered), your phone's ad settings, or your Google account's My Ad Center.
  • Data held by us: we hold none from the app, so there is nothing for us to delete or export. Email us if you have a question about this.

Security

App data is kept in Android's private app storage. BACnet itself is an unencrypted local protocol with no login; anyone on the same network can read the same devices, so treat the building network — and any exported points list — with care.
