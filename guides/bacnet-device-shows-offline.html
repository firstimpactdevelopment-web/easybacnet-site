Why does my BACnet IP device show offline?

Updated 3 October 2026

Short answer

“Offline” in BACnet means I stopped hearing from it, which is usually a network problem rather than dead hardware. In order of likelihood: your phone is on a different subnet so the broadcast never reaches the device, the Wi-Fi is dropping broadcasts, a firewall or access list is blocking UDP 47808, two devices share a Device ID, or the device really has lost power or its network link. Before any of that, make sure you are looking at a device that does not answer, and not at a point with a fault flag, which is a different problem.

Offline or fault?

People use “offline” for two different things. A device that does not answer discovery or reads is a communication problem, and the rest of this guide is about that. A device that answers but has a point showing Fault or an impossible value has a sensor or wiring problem on the equipment. If you can open the device and read its points, it is not offline. See status flags explained for the fault case.

Work through these in order

1. Is your phone on the same subnet?

Controls equipment normally sits on its own subnet or VLAN. A phone on guest Wi-Fi or the office network never receives the device's reply, so everything looks offline. When a scan finds nothing, the app shows the phone's IP and mask; compare them with the device's address. If they differ, see what is a subnet, and why doesn't the switch give me the right one? A device on another subnet needs a BBMD or a direct add by IP (step 3) to be seen at all.

2. Scan again

Discovery is a broadcast, and Wi-Fi access points drop broadcasts under load. A device that shows up in one scan and is gone in the next is usually this. The app repeats the Who-Is through each scan and then tries each address directly, but a second scan, or a wired connection, settles it.

3. Add it directly by IP

Menu → Add device by IP… sends a directed message instead of a broadcast. If the device answers that, it is online and the problem is purely that broadcasts are not getting through: another subnet, client isolation, or a switch that filters them. If it does not answer, carry on.

4. Is UDP 47808 blocked, or is the device on another port?

BACnet/IP uses UDP 47808. An access list between VLANs, or a device set to 47809 or higher, keeps it unreachable even from the right subnet. The port can be typed in when adding by IP; the access list is a question for the network team. See what port does BACnet use?

5. Does another device share its Device ID?

Two devices with the same ID, or two MS/TP devices with the same MAC behind one router, answer for each other. The symptom is a device that flickers between online and offline, or whose name and IP change between scans. The scan summary warns about both. See duplicate Device IDs.

6. It really is down

Lost power, a pulled cable, a dead switch port, or an MS/TP trunk that has stopped behind its router. Once the steps above are ruled out, this is what is left, and it is time to look at the equipment. A ping from a laptop on the same network confirms it: see is my BACnet device online?
