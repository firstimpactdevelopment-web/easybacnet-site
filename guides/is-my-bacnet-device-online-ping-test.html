How do I tell if a BACnet device is really online before blaming the app?

Updated 3 October 2026

Short answer

Two tests settle it. First, in Easy BACnet, use Add device by IP… with the controller's address: that contacts it directly, with no broadcast involved, and if it answers the device is online and your scan is being blocked by the network. If it does not answer, ping the address from a laptop on the same network. A failed ping means the network path is broken (wrong subnet, firewall, cable, wrong IP). A successful ping with no BACnet answer means the device is up but BACnet is blocked or misconfigured: the wrong port, a firewall that allows ping but not UDP 47808, or a device problem.

Why test this way

A scan can fail for many reasons, and most of them have nothing to do with the device. These two tests separate the possibilities. A directed BACnet read proves the device is alive and speaking BACnet. A ping proves only that packets can reach the address. Running them in that order tells you whether you are dealing with a broadcast problem, a network problem, or a BACnet configuration problem, and therefore who to call.

Step 1 — find the device's IP address

If the device has ever appeared in a scan, its IP is shown under its name. If not, look on the controller's label, its front-panel display, or the commissioning sheet; ask the controls contractor; or ask the network team to read it from the switch's DHCP or ARP table.

Step 2 — add it by IP in the app

In Easy BACnet Mode, menu (three dots) → Add device by IP…, type the address and port (47808 unless the site uses another) and tap Add. The app sends a directed Who-Is and a direct read.

  • It answers. The device is online and speaking BACnet. Your scan failed because the broadcast is not reaching it: you are on another subnet, the Wi-Fi has client isolation on, or a switch filters broadcasts. For the whole remote network you need the site's BBMD; for this one device, you are done.
  • It does not answer (No BACnet IP device answered at…). Go to step 3.

Step 3 — ping it

Ping from a device on the same network as the controller; a laptop plugged into the controls switch is ideal. Open a command prompt and type ping and the address, for example ping 10.20.30.41. A ping app on the phone does the same job. You want replies, not “request timed out” or “destination unreachable”.

The ping fails

The device cannot be reached on the network, so no BACnet tool will see it until that is fixed. Causes, most likely first:

  • You are on a different subnet with no route to it. Get onto the controls network and try again.
  • The IP is wrong, or the device moved to a new DHCP address.
  • A cable is unplugged, a switch port is dead, or the unit is powered down.
  • A firewall or VLAN access list blocks the path. Some block ping on purpose while allowing everything else, so a failed ping on a managed network is not final proof.

This is an IT / network conversation, unless you can see the device itself is unpowered.

The ping succeeds, but step 2 failed

The device is on the network but not answering BACnet at that address and port. Possible reasons:

  • Wrong UDP port. Sites running several BACnet networks on one wire use 47809 and up. Ping ignores ports; BACnet does not. Try the add again with the right port. See what port does BACnet use?
  • A firewall allows ping but blocks UDP 47808. They are separate rules and it is common to have one without the other. Network team.
  • The address belongs to a router, not the device. An MS/TP controller has no IP of its own; the IP you have may be its router, which answers ping but only forwards BACnet for trunks that are configured. See does Easy BACnet support MS/TP?
  • BACnet is disabled or misconfigured on the device, or it shares a Device ID with another unit. Controls contractor.

Both work, but the device shows no points or no values

Communication is fine. The device is slow to enumerate, refusing some property reads, or has very little to show. See the app can't find what I'm looking for.

What to tell whoever you call

One sentence with both results saves a lot of back-and-forth: “10.20.30.41 pings from a laptop on the controls switch, but a directed BACnet read on port 47808 gets no answer.” That rules out the cable, the power and the network path, and points at the port, a firewall rule, or the device's BACnet settings.
