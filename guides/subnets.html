Understanding networks and subnets

The one thing to know

Being connected to the Wi-Fi is not the same as being on the building's control network. A building often runs several separate networks over the same wiring, and the BACnet equipment usually lives on its own. If a scan finds nothing, the most likely reason is simply that this device is on a different network than the controllers — not that anything is broken.

Why "on the Wi-Fi" isn't always enough

Think of a large building as having several separate mail systems that happen to share the same hallways. The guest Wi-Fi is one system, the office computers are another, and the heating, cooling and ventilation controls are often a third. Each of these is called a subnet. A message sent on one subnet does not automatically reach the others.

A BACnet scan works by sending out a broadcast — the equivalent of calling out "is anyone there?" — and listening for equipment to answer. That call only travels within the subnet you are on. If the controllers are on the controls subnet and your device joined the guest Wi-Fi, they never hear the call, and the scan comes back empty even though everything is working normally.

How to tell which network you are on

When a scan finds nothing, Easy BACnet shows this device's network address (for example 192.168.1.42). The first three groups of numbers usually identify the subnet. If the controllers are known to be on, say, 10.20.30.x, and your address starts with 192.168.1.x, you are on a different subnet and that is why nothing was found.

What to do about it

  • Share the details. Give the network address shown in the app to your network and HVAC provider and ask which network the building's BACnet system is on, and how to connect to it.
  • Ask about a wired connection. A network port in a mechanical room or control panel is often on the correct subnet already.
  • Ask whether a BBMD is available. Some buildings have a device that forwards these broadcasts between subnets. Your provider will know.
  • Use a known address. If you have a controller's IP address, Advanced Mode can connect to it directly with Add device, which works across subnets when routing allows it.

Other things that can quietly block a scan

  • Guest Wi-Fi isolation. Many guest networks deliberately stop devices from talking to anything else on the network.
  • Wi-Fi dropping broadcasts. Wireless can be unreliable for this kind of traffic; a wired connection is more dependable.
  • A firewall on a laptop or on the network can block the traffic BACnet uses.

None of these are problems with the equipment or with Easy BACnet — they are about which network you are connected to. Your network and HVAC provider can confirm the right connection for the building.
