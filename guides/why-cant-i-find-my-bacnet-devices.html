Why can't I find my BACnet IP devices when I scan?

Updated 11 October 2026

Short answer

Almost always because the Who-Is broadcast isn't reaching the controllers, or their replies aren't reaching you. A broadcast only covers your own IP subnet, so first rule out being on the office or guest network instead of the controls network. After that: Wi-Fi that drops or blocks broadcasts, devices on a non-standard UDP port, or an access list blocking 47808. When a scan finds nothing at all, it's rarely the controllers.

Check these in order

1. Are you on the controls network?

Broadcasts don't cross routers, so you have to be on the same subnet as the controllers. When a scan finds nothing, Easy BACnet shows the phone's own IP address and mask. Compare that with a controller's address (from its label, display, the JACE/head-end, or the as-builts). With a 255.255.255.0 mask the first three numbers must match: a phone on 10.20.30.250 can find a controller on 10.20.30.41 but not one on 192.168.1.41.

If they don't match, you're on the wrong network. Building Wi-Fi is the usual culprit, because controls normally sit on their own VLAN that isn't on Wi-Fi at all. The reliable way in is a USB-C Ethernet adapter into a port on the controls switch, often the one in the JACE or supervisory panel. An address starting 169.254 means no DHCP server answered, which is normal on controls networks; you need a static address in the controls range. See what is a subnet, and why doesn't the switch give me the right one?

On Wi-Fi that shows Connected, no internet, turn mobile data off and scan again so Android doesn't route around it.

2. Is the network blocking broadcasts?

Wi-Fi often drops broadcast traffic, and corporate or guest Wi-Fi usually has client isolation on, which stops you reaching other devices at all. Easy BACnet re-sends Who-Is during the scan, and if nothing has answered after a few seconds it contacts each address on the subnet directly. Devices found that way are reported as having only answered when contacted directly: the network is blocking broadcast replies, and most other BACnet tools on that connection will see nothing. A wired connection on the controls switch fixes it.

3. Are the devices on a non-standard port?

The standard is UDP 47808 (0xBAC0). Sites running a second BACnet network on the same wire sometimes use 47809 or nearby. Easy BACnet also tries 47809, 47810 and 47817 during a scan. For anything else, use Add device by IP… and type the port.

4. Is an access list in the way?

A router or firewall between VLANs can block UDP 47808 even when the addressing is right. If the first three checks pass, this is a question for IT.

If you find some devices but not others

  • They're on another subnet. The ones you found share your subnet; the missing ones are behind a router. Add one by IP, or register with the site's BBMD (below).
  • A whole MS/TP trunk is missing. MS/TP devices answer through their BACnet router, showing a network number and MAC instead of their own IP. If every device on a trunk is missing, look at the router or the trunk itself (wiring, baud rate, a duplicate MAC). That's a controls job.
  • The device is down. Check power and the link light first.
  • Two devices share a Device ID. One can seem to vanish, or the two trade places between scans. Easy BACnet warns about duplicates after a scan. See duplicate Device IDs.

If a device shows no name or no points

It answered discovery but its property reads are failing or slow. Give a large controller a minute, then back out and open it again. A device behind a busy MS/TP router can take a while. If the scan marks it needs commissioning, it's reporting Device ID 4194303, which isn't a valid ID, and it needs setting up before anything can address it reliably.

Reaching devices on another subnet

Two options in the menu (three dots, top right):

  • Add device by IP… contacts one controller directly. It crosses routers because it isn't a broadcast, so it works as long as the phone can reach that address.
  • BBMD for remote networks… registers the phone with the site's BBMD as a foreign device so scans reach the subnets it serves. Get the BBMD's address from whoever manages the controls network.
