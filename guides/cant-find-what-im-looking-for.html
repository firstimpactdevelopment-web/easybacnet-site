The app can't find what I'm looking for — what do I do?

Updated 3 October 2026

Short answer

Usually the phone is not on the same network as the equipment. When a scan finds nothing, the app shows the phone's IP address and subnet mask: if the first three numbers do not match the controllers' addresses, join the building's controls Wi-Fi or plug in with a USB-Ethernet adapter and scan again. If the addresses do match, or the problem is a missing device, an empty point list or a blank value, find the matching section below.

The scan finds nothing at all

You tapped Scan for Devices and got the red No devices found banner. Work down this list; each step is cheaper than the next.

  1. Compare addresses. Under the banner the app lists the phone's IP address and mask. Find a controller's address on its label, in its front-panel menu, or in the hand-over documents. With a 255.255.255.0 mask the first three numbers must match (phone 192.168.1.20, controller 192.168.1.50). If they do not, nothing else matters until you are on the right network.
  2. Get on the right network. Building controls are almost always on their own network or VLAN, not the office or guest Wi-Fi. Ask whoever runs the site which network the controllers are on and how to join it. If there is no Wi-Fi in the plant room, plug a USB-Ethernet adapter into the phone and run a cable to the same switch the controllers use.
  3. Turn mobile data off if the status bar says Connected, no internet, then scan again.
  4. Scan once more. Wi-Fi drops the broadcast a scan relies on. The app repeats it through the scan and then tries each address on the subnet directly, but a second run sometimes catches what the first missed. A wired connection removes this problem entirely.
  5. Add the device by IP. If you know a controller's address, menu → Add device by IP… contacts it directly without a broadcast. If that works when a scan does not, the device is fine and the network is blocking broadcasts: you are on another segment, or the Wi-Fi has client isolation on. Ask the network team for a port on the controls VLAN, or for the site's BBMD address (menu → BBMD for remote networks…).
  6. Ping it. If even a directed add fails, ping the address from a laptop on the same network. No reply means the network path is the problem; a reply with no BACnet answer means the device is up but BACnet is blocked or misconfigured. It finds some devices, but not the one I want

  • Look at the Device ID, not just the name. Every device shows its ID and IP address under the name. The unit you want may have a blank or unhelpful name and the right ID.
  • Check it is powered and plugged in. A controller with a dead link light cannot answer.
  • It may be on another segment. Devices near you answered; this one is behind a router. Add it by IP, plug into its switch, or use a BBMD.
  • It may be an MS/TP device whose router is not forwarding. Those devices appear only through a BACnet router. If the whole trunk is missing, that is a controls question. See does Easy BACnet support MS/TP?
  • Two devices may share an ID. The scan summary warns about duplicates; one of the pair can appear to vanish. See duplicate Device IDs.

I opened a device but it shows no points

  • Give it a minute. The app reads one object at a time, so a large controller takes a while. Watch the Loading points counter.
  • Back out and open it again. One dropped reply can stall the list; reopening starts fresh.
  • Small devices have few points. A sensor may expose one or two. That is the device, not a fault.
  • A huge controller shows only part of its list. The app loads the first 2,000 objects and says so. Reach the rest with menu → Add object by number (how).

I see a point but no value

  • Tap Refresh. Values are read live; one missed reply shows a dash until the next read.
  • If every point on the device shows dashes, it has stopped answering. Scan again to confirm it is still there.
  • Some points have no present value. Schedules, notification classes and similar objects are settings, not readings.

Who to call

In short: if you cannot reach the equipment on the network, call IT / network and have the phone's IP and mask ready. If you can reach it but the points, IDs or values are wrong, call the controls / BMS contractor and send them the exported spreadsheet. If the point reads correctly but the machine is not doing what it says, call the HVAC / mechanical contractor. The full breakdown, with what to have ready for each, is in who do I call — IT, HVAC, or controls/BMS?
