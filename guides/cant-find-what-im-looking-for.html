The app can't find what I'm looking for — what do I do?

Updated 11 October 2026

Short answer

Usually the phone is not on the same network as the controllers. When a scan finds nothing, the app shows the phone's IP address and subnet mask. If they don't put you in the same subnet as the controllers (with a 255.255.255.0 mask, the first three numbers must match), get onto the controls network, usually with a USB-C Ethernet adapter into the controls switch, and scan again. If the addresses do match, or the problem is a missing device, an empty point list or a blank value, find the matching section below.

The scan finds nothing at all

You tapped Scan for Devices and got the red No devices found banner. Work down this list, cheapest checks first.

  1. Compare addresses. Under the banner the app lists the phone's IP address and mask. Get a controller's address from the head-end's device list, the as-built drawings or the controller's label. With a 255.255.255.0 mask the first three numbers must match (phone 192.168.10.20, controller 192.168.10.50). If they don't, nothing else matters until you are on the right network.
  2. Get on the right network. Building controls are usually on their own VLAN or a separate flat network, not the office or guest Wi-Fi. Ask IT or the controls contractor which network and IP range the controllers use. The reliable way in is a USB-C Ethernet adapter and a cable to the controls switch, with a free static IP in that range (check with whoever owns it before you pick one).
  3. Turn mobile data off if the status bar says Connected, no internet, then scan again.
  4. Scan once more. Broadcasts get lost, particularly on Wi-Fi. The app repeats the Who-Is during the scan and then asks each address on the subnet directly, but a second run sometimes catches what the first missed. A wired connection is far more reliable.
  5. Add the device by IP. If you know a controller's address, menu → Add device by IP… contacts it directly without a broadcast. If that works when a scan does not, the device is fine and the broadcast isn't reaching it: it is on another subnet, or the Wi-Fi has client isolation on. Ask for a port on the controls VLAN, or for the site's BBMD address (menu → BBMD for remote networks…).
  6. Ping it. If even a directed add fails, ping the address from a laptop or a ping app on the same network. No reply points at the network path; a reply with no BACnet answer means the device is up but BACnet isn't getting through. Is my BACnet device online? walks through what each result means.

It finds some devices, but not the one I want

  • Look at the Device ID, not just the name. Every device shows its ID and IP address under the name. The unit you want may have a default name like the controller model, with the right ID.
  • Check it is powered and connected. A controller with no link light on its Ethernet port can't answer.
  • It may be on another subnet. Devices near you answered; this one is behind an IP router. Add it by IP, plug into its switch, or use the site's BBMD.
  • It may be an MS/TP device whose router isn't passing it through. MS/TP devices only appear via a BACnet router. If a whole trunk is missing, that is a controls question (router config, a trunk wiring fault, or a bad device dragging the trunk down). See does Easy BACnet support MS/TP?
  • Two devices may share an ID. Easy BACnet lists both and flags them duplicate ID, but the head-end and other tools often show only one and talk to whichever answers first. See duplicate Device IDs.

I opened a device but it shows no points

  • Give it a minute. The app reads one property at a time, so a large controller takes a while. Watch the Loading points counter.
  • Back out and open it again. A run of dropped replies can stop the load; reopening starts it fresh.
  • Small devices have few objects. A thermostat or a meter gateway may expose only a handful. That is the device, not a fault.
  • A big controller shows only part of its list. The app loads the first 2,000 objects and says so. Reach the rest with menu → Add object by number (how).

I see a point but no value

  • Tap Refresh. Values are read live; one missed reply shows a dash until the next read.
  • If every point on the device shows dashes, it has stopped answering. Scan again to confirm it is still there.
  • Some objects have no Present_Value. Trend logs, notification classes, programs and files hold configuration or history, not a live reading.

Who to call

In short: if you can't reach the equipment on the network, call IT / network and have the phone's IP and mask ready. If you can reach it but the points, IDs or values are wrong, call the controls / BAS contractor and send them the exported spreadsheet. If the point reads correctly but the equipment isn't doing what it says, call the HVAC / mechanical contractor. The full breakdown, with what to have ready for each, is in who do I call — IT, HVAC, or controls/BMS?
