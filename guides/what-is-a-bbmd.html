What is a BBMD, and when do I need one?

Updated 11 October 2026

Short answer

A BBMD (BACnet Broadcast Management Device) forwards BACnet/IP broadcasts from one IP subnet to another. Discovery depends on broadcasts, and IP routers do not pass them, so equipment on another subnet stays invisible to a Who-Is until a BBMD carries it across. You need one on each subnet whenever a single BACnet IP network spans more than one IP subnet. In practice it is usually a function switched on in a JACE, a supervisory controller or a BACnet router, not a separate box.

Why broadcasts are the problem

A Who-Is is a broadcast: it reaches every device on the local subnet in one shot. Routers stop broadcasts at the subnet edge by design. So when the RTU controllers are on 10.20.30.0/24 and the head-end is on 10.20.40.0/24, discovery stops at the router. Directed (unicast) reads still cross it fine; only the broadcast part breaks.

What a BBMD does

One BBMD sits on each subnet that has BACnet/IP devices, and each holds a Broadcast Distribution Table (BDT) listing its peers. When a broadcast appears on subnet A, A's BBMD sends it as a unicast to the BBMDs in its table, and each one re-broadcasts it on its own subnet. A Who-Is on subnet A is then heard on subnet B without opening the routers to broadcast traffic.

The usual commissioning mistakes: BDTs that do not match on every BBMD, two BBMDs on the same subnet (broadcasts get duplicated), and a firewall between subnets that drops the unicast UDP 47808 traffic between BBMDs.

Foreign Device Registration

A device that is not on a BACnet subnet, such as a laptop on a VPN or a tool on another VLAN, can register with a BBMD as a foreign device. The BBMD forwards broadcasts to it for a set time-to-live, and the tool hands its own broadcasts to the BBMD to distribute. The tool has to re-register before the time runs out. Some BBMDs have foreign-device registration turned off, or limit how many may register.

What this means for a phone scan

Easy BACnet scans the subnet the phone is on. If the equipment is elsewhere and no BBMD is set up for your phone, the scan finds nothing because the broadcast never crossed the router. On site the dependable fix is to get the phone onto the same subnet as the controllers; see why can't I find my BACnet IP devices?.

Scanning through a BBMD with Easy BACnet

If the site has a BBMD and your phone can reach its IP address, Easy BACnet can register with it as a foreign device:

  1. In Easy BACnet Mode, open the menu (three dots) → BBMD for remote networks….
  2. Enter the BBMD IP address and its UDP port (47808 is standard), then tap Save. The app confirms Scans will now also go through the BBMD at … and remembers the address.
  3. Tap Scan for Devices. Each scan registers the phone with the BBMD and sends its Who-Is through it, as well as on the local subnet.

If the BBMD rejects the registration you get a warning with its result code; ask whoever manages it to allow foreign-device registration. If it does not answer at all, the scan covered the local network only. Tap Clear in the same dialog to stop using it. For a single known controller, add it by IP instead.
